01ABOUT THE BRAND

Who we are.
Why it matters.

The Upskilling Labs is a citizen R&D community — not a course, not a platform. It's a working lab where professionals build real skills by doing real work with real people. The model is simple: show up, find a pod, work on something that matters.

Launched in January 2026 at the DC Public Library, over 300 people have come through programs, platforms, and mentorship. The format is replicable — designed so any civic community across the US can adopt it.

When you create materials under this brand, you're representing that. Every flyer, every post, every slide is an extension of a community that takes the work seriously. The rules in this guide protect that identity. Use them.

How to describe The Labs in one sentence

"The Upskilling Labs is a citizen R&D community that helps professionals build hands-on AI skills through peer-powered cohorts — rooted in local libraries."

Shorten only to "The Labs" — never "TUL", not even in filenames.

Design lineage

Our layout and typographic standards are deeply inspired by NASA's 1976 Graphics Standards Manual (Danne & Blackburn) and the International Typographic Style behind it: a strict grid, systematic type scale, restrained palette with a single red accent — and type set flush left, ragged right, never centered.

The orb mark is a homage to the NASA "meatball." Expressive round emblem, disciplined flush-left system.

A homage to the NASA meatball

The orb is a direct homage to NASA's "meatball" insignia — a round emblem with a vector swoosh cutting across it. Ours reads the same way: a circular field, a teal upper hemisphere over a red lower lobe, and a single teal swoosh. It is the round counterpart to a systematic, flush-left brand — expressive mark, disciplined grid.

05CONTENT LANGUAGE

Voice & Tone

Warm, plain, human. Written by someone who knows what they're talking about and believes the reader is capable of more than they think. Not corporate, not winking.

Second-person "you"
"You'll work in pods" — not "Participants work in pods." Speak directly to the reader. No royal "we."
Specific nouns
"Your local library," "the twelve-week Build Cycle," "your pod," "Upskiller." Not "community" or "platform." Never "course," "class," or "student."
Real verbs
Iterating, shipping, building, navigating. Never "unlock," "leverage," "revolutionize," or "empower."
Declarative imperatives
Short stacked lines at the top of anything. "Find your people. Build your edge." Parallel lines, period at the end.
No hype or emoji
No exclamation points in marketing copy. No emoji. The brand earns excitement through specificity, not punctuation.
Plain present tense
"We run a working lab." Not "We will empower you to unlock your potential." Plain, declarative, confident.
Signature patterns — use these verbatim
Hero
"Find your people. Build your edge." — the shipped landing headline and site tagline. Two stacked lines, period at the end.
Subhead
"The Labs isn't a class you sit through. It's where you practice becoming the person you want to be — on real problems, with people who notice."
CTA
"Join The Labs" — red filled on the landing hero, teal-deep elsewhere — never "Sign up" or "Get started"
Footer
"A commons for upskilling — learn by doing, in the open. Projects, playbooks, and lessons, built like open source."
Naming
"The Labs" is the only shortening of The Upskilling Labs. Never "TUL" — not in copy, not in filenames.
Words we never use
seamless powerful supercharge revolutionize amazing awesome unlock leverage empower game-changing cutting-edge innovative disruptive transformative

Radial rule — the most important spatial constraint

Teal radiates outward from corners and edges. The logo, orb, and headlines always sit over a dark ink center. Never place a teal blob in the middle of a composition. Dark ink at center → teal at edges is the rule.

Focus & inputs

Every interactive element shows a visible focus ring: 2px #0094A0 outline, 2px offset. Inputs are 16px minimum (smaller triggers iOS zoom). Never remove focus styles.

Respect prefers-reduced-motion — drop non-essential animation when it's set. Always write alt text; the logo's alt is "The Upskilling Labs."

10GRID SYSTEM

Grid & Structure

A flush-left grid on a 4px baseline — every spacing value is a multiple of 4. Everything hangs from the left rail; lines break ragged right. A handful of real tokens, not a ten-step scale. Sections breathe: 56px vertical on mobile, 72px on desktop.

The flush-left grid
12 columns · the solid rail is the left edge everything aligns to · 24→40px margins

Everything hangs from the left rail. Text sets flush left and breaks ragged right — never centered, never justified. Columns divide the content width; the reading measure caps at 760px so prose never runs too wide in the full-width shell.

This is the discipline of the 1976 NASA Graphics Standards Manual, in the brand's own tokens: a systematic column grid, a single flush-left alignment, and margins that always land on the 4px baseline.

14REFERENCE

Do's & Don'ts

The most common mistakes and their correct alternatives.

✓
Do
Set type flush left, ragged right — aligned to the grid's left rail
Use Geologica at every size — display, body, caption, eyebrow (weights 400–700 only)
Start pages on warm paper #F6F4EF — dark is reserved for covers, nav bars, and footers
One radius — 14px on every rectangular container; circles only for avatars, dots, pips
Place the logo on dark ink only, with generous clear space
Add SVG grain overlay to every dark or gradient surface
Let teal radiate outward from corners — never from the center
Write second-person "you," plain present tense, specific nouns
Break between dark and light sections — never stack two dark
Use real typographic glyphs — em-dash (—) en-dash (–) middot (·)
✗
Don't
Center or justify headlines or body copy — type is always flush left
Use any typeface other than Geologica — no Inter, no Arial
Use pill shapes (999px) or boxed capsule eyebrows — the one radius is 14px
Abbreviate to "TUL" — the only shortening is "The Labs"
Place the logo on teal, red, white, or any light background
Center a teal gradient blob behind the logo or headline
Use gradient backgrounds inside cards, or left-border accent strips
Use emoji, exclamation points, or the forbidden word list
Use photos with horizon lines, stairs, blinds, or warm tones
Drop shadows on text or the logo — cards use the two soft ambient shadow tokens only
